# Study Plan

What to read, in what order. Holds no content of its own — everything here points into
`Navigation.md`, `Instruments Inventory.md`, or the cartography and lighthouse maps.

## 1. Order

1. **Start with the problem**, `Navigation.md` §1 — why latitude is easy and longitude is not.
   Without this the rest reads as unmotivated technique.
2. **Dead reckoning**, §3. It comes first in practice because it is always available, and
   everything else is a correction to it.
3. **Meridian altitude**, §2.1, then the instruments that take it — `Instruments Inventory.md`
   §1, in the order listed, which is chronological.
4. **Errors before more methods**, §5. A navigator who knows three methods and no corrections
   is more dangerous than one who knows a single method well.
5. **Sight reduction**, §2.3, last of the celestial material. Note the warning about a tight
   triangle: it is the one idea in the section that is not arithmetic.
6. **Charts**, `../cartography/Cartography.md`, once position-finding makes sense. Projections
   are easier to care about after you have plotted something.
7. **Lights**, `../lighthouses/Lighthouses.md`, for the landfall case specifically.

## 2. Open questions

- The visibility disagreement between `Navigation.md` §5.2 and `Lighthouses.md` §3.1 — one
  half against one third — is unresolved. Neither document cites a source. Worth settling
  before either figure gets used for planning.
- Lunar distance is described as superseded, but nothing here records how a chronometer's
  rate was actually checked at sea over a long passage. Gap.

## 3. Deliberately skipped

Ornamental instrument history. Biographies. The longitude prize as a story rather than a method.
